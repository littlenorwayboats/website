# Exploring Liberty Bay: A Boaters' Guide to Poulsbo's Local Wildlife

Gliding out from the Poulsbo Marina into the calm, sheltered waters of Liberty Bay offers front-row access to one of Puget Sound's richest marine ecosystems. Whether you are paddling a kayak along the shoreline or cruising toward Agate Pass aboard a [Little Norway harbor rental](/logbook/liberty-bay-boat-rental-guide/), keep a sharp eye on the water, pilings, and treetops. The bay reveals a vibrant cast of local wildlife.

**Learn more about common species:** [Harbor seals](https://www.eopugetsound.org/articles/harbor-seals) (Encyclopedia of Puget Sound) · [North American river otter](https://crittercarewildlife.org/river-otter/) (Critter Care Wildlife Society) · [Bufflehead](https://travisaudubon.org/news/bird-of-the-week-bufflehead/) (Travis Audubon) · [Purple martin](https://www.nwf.org/Magazines/National-Wildlife/2004/Become-a-Purple-Martin-Landlord) (National Wildlife Federation)

## Marine Mammals

### Harbor Seals

The undisputed stars of Liberty Bay. Because the bay is a protected pupping ground, seeing curious, spotted heads bobbing near your boat—or spotting groups hauled out on log booms and sandbars—is virtually guaranteed year-round.

### North American River Otters

Despite their name, local river otters thrive in the saltwater of Puget Sound. Look for sleek, dark brown swimmers slipping off docks and rocky shorelines, often traveling in playful family groups.

### Orcas (Killer Whales)

While extremely infrequent, Bigg's (transient) orcas do occasionally make headlines by slipping through Agate Pass and touring Liberty Bay in search of seals. Catching a glimpse of these apex predators in the bay is a once-in-a-lifetime local sighting.

## Coastal & Diving Birds

### Bald Eagles

Nearly impossible to miss, bald eagles perch like sentinels atop the tall Douglas firs ringing the bay and swoop low over the water to snatch fish.

![Bald eagle soaring over Liberty Bay near Poulsbo.](/images/blog/liberty-bay-eagle.png)

### Bufflehead Ducks

Arriving in the cooler months, these compact, striking diving ducks are a winter staple. Males sport an iridescent green-and-purple head with a bold white patch, while females are sleek gray-brown with a white cheek spot.

### Purple Martins

Look for North America's largest swallows from spring through late summer. Local conservation efforts have placed specialized gourd and wooden nest boxes on pilings throughout Liberty Bay, where you can watch them swoop gracefully over the surface catching insects.

### Great Blue Herons

Often seen standing motionless like statues in the shallow tideflats near Dogfish Creek and Waterfront Park, waiting to spear small fish.

### Double-Crested Cormorants & Ospreys

Cormorants frequently dry their outstretched wings on marina pilings, while ospreys visit in the warmer months to plunge feet-first into the bay for fish.

### Pigeon Guillemots & Mergansers

Watch for pigeon guillemots flashing bright red feet as they dive near rocky banks, alongside flocks of red-breasted and common mergansers patrolling the shoreline.

## Beneath the Surface & Along the Shore

### Lion's Mane & Moon Jellyfish

Peer straight down over the gunwale on calm days to spot translucent moon jellies and dramatic, trailing orange lion's mane jellyfish pulsing just beneath the surface.

### Dungeness & Kelp Crabs

In the shallow eelgrass beds and around marina docks, Dungeness and kelp crabs scuttle along the bottom, sharing the habitat with colorful ochre and mottled sea stars.

### Chum & Coho Salmon

In the fall, returning salmon ripple the surface of the bay as they stage near the mouth of Dogfish Creek before heading upstream to spawn. You can visit Poulsbo's fish park to catch a glipmse of the action.
