# How to Get to Poulsbo from Seattle: Ferry vs. Driving

Tucked away on the Kitsap Peninsula, Poulsbo ("Little Norway") feels worlds apart from the bustle of Seattle—yet getting there is half the adventure. Whether you’re coming straight from Sea-Tac Airport or heading out from downtown, you have two main ways to reach town: taking the ferry or driving around.

Both routes offer classic Pacific Northwest scenery, but depending on your schedule, budget, and travel style, one might make a lot more sense than the other.

## Option 1: The Bainbridge Island Ferry

If timed right, the ferry can be faster, more relaxing, and much more scenic than driving. From downtown Seattle, the ferry takes you across Puget Sound to Bainbridge Island, followed by a quick 20-minute drive north to Poulsbo. (If you’re coming from Sea-Tac Airport, it’s only about a 15-minute drive to the downtown terminal.)

![Father and child watching the Bainbridge Island shoreline through a ferry window at sunset.](/images/blog/child-looking-out-ferry-window-at-sunset.jpg)

### Good to know before you go

- **Fares & walk-ons:** Walk-on passengers pay around $10 heading from Seattle to Bainbridge, and the return trip to Seattle as a walk-on is free. Driving a standard vehicle onto the boat costs around $28 (prices vary slightly by season and vehicle size).
- **Airport hack:** Traveling without a car? You can get from Bainbridge Island all the way to Sea-Tac Airport for about $3 by walking onto the ferry to Seattle and hopping on the Link Light Rail at Pioneer Square.
- **Timing & apps:** Vehicle space fills up fast—especially on summer weekends, during rush hour, and on Sunday afternoons heading back to Seattle. Walk-on passengers almost never get turned away. Download the FerryFriend app to keep an eye on live schedules and boat capacities.

### The pros

- **Breaks up the drive:** You spend much less time behind the wheel.
- **Classic PNW experience:** Step outside for sweeping views of the Seattle skyline and Puget Sound—great for photos and spotting local sea life.
- **Cozy onboard amenities:** Grab a local beer, coffee, or hot chocolate from the galley and watch the rain roll across the water, or work on the community jigsaw puzzles laid out on the tables (a huge hit with kids).

### The cons

- **Unpredictable waits:** Ferry delays happen. If you just miss a boat or the car deck fills up, you could be waiting in line for 45 minutes or more for the next sailing.
- **Higher cost for drivers:** Taking a car across the water costs significantly more than paying the bridge toll on the driving route.
- **Limited rideshares on the other side:** Uber and Lyft are much less reliable in Kitsap County, especially at night. If you walk on, plan ahead to use Kitsap Transit buses (such as Route 390 to Poulsbo) or arrange a local pickup.

## Option 2: Driving Around via the Tacoma Narrows Bridge

If you prefer to stay in control of your own schedule, you can drive south around Puget Sound and up the Kitsap Peninsula. Depending on traffic, the drive takes roughly 1.5 to 2 hours.

![Rainbow over the highway near Gig Harbor on the drive toward Poulsbo, with evergreen forest along the roadside.](/images/blog/driving-past-gig-harbor-with-rainbow.jpg)

### What the drive looks like

You’ll head south past Tacoma and cross the Tacoma Narrows Bridge (famous as the replacement for the historic 1940 “Galloping Gertie” bridge, which collapsed in high winds). Once across, you’ll pass the naval base in Bremerton and cruise up a smooth, two-lane highway lined with towering evergreen trees straight toward Poulsbo.

**Navigation tip:** Depending on the time of day, Google Maps or Apple Maps will often default to routing you onto the ferry. If you want to drive around, add Tacoma or Gig Harbor as a temporary stop to force the route south, then remove the stop once you’re on your way. (In your map settings, you can also toggle “Avoid ferries” on and off.)

**Arrival tip:** Take the Finn Hill Road exit as you enter Poulsbo—it brings you right past the iconic Viking statue and straight through downtown.

### The pros

- **Predictable ETA:** No worrying about sailing schedules or boat capacity—your GPS will give you a reliable arrival time.
- **Easy highway cruising:** Once you clear Tacoma, it’s a smooth, scenic stretch of road where you can easily set the cruise control.

### The cons

- **I-5 traffic:** Heavy congestion through Seattle and Tacoma can easily turn a 90-minute drive into two hours.
- **More time behind the wheel:** You’re driving the entire way, and you will need to pay a toll crossing the Tacoma Narrows Bridge (eastbound toward Tacoma).

## Other Ways to Get There

Depending on where you're starting from or the time of year, you have a couple of alternate water routes:

- **Edmonds–Kingston Ferry:** If you’re coming from north of Seattle, take the ferry from Edmonds to Kingston. From the Kingston terminal, it’s an easy 20-minute drive straight into Poulsbo.
- **Seasonal Seattle–Poulsbo fast ferry:** During select holiday seasons, the Victoria Clipper has offered special passenger-only sailings directly between downtown Seattle and the Poulsbo waterfront—worth checking if you're visiting around the holidays. See our [Christmas in Poulsbo guide](/logbook/poulsbo-christmas-holiday-guide/) for current seasonal details.

## Which Route Should You Choose?

**Take the ferry** if you have a flexible schedule, want to stretch your legs, or are visiting with family and want the boat ride to be part of the vacation.

**Drive around** if you’re on a strict timeline, traveling late at night, or trying to catch an early morning flight out of Sea-Tac.

The ultimate recommendation? If you’re visiting for a few days, do both—take the ferry over to kick off your trip in true Pacific Northwest style, and drive back around when you’re ready to head home.

Once you’re in town, rent an [electric boat on Liberty Bay](/logbook/liberty-bay-boat-rental-guide/) or spend a day exploring [downtown Poulsbo with kids](/logbook/downtown-poulsbo-with-kids/).
